# Commercial Analytics — Planning, Variance, Pricing and Forecasting

This document covers the commercial planning layer: what it does, how each
number is derived, what the data supports, and — just as importantly — what it
does not support and why.

Everything here is built by `sql/duckdb/08_commercial_planning.sql`,
`sql/duckdb/09_quality_commercial.sql`, `src/forecasting.py` and
`src/build_plan_source.py`, and is rebuilt by `python src/run_all.py --rebuild`.

---

## 1. The second source system

The sales side of this warehouse has one source: seven CSV extracts. A
commercial function has at least two, because **plan data does not come from
the transactional system**. It comes from regional teams, in spreadsheets, in
local currency, late, and with the spelling conventions of whoever typed it.

This project therefore adds a second source — `data/planning/regional_plan_submissions.csv`
— and puts it through the same `raw → stg → core → mart → qa` discipline as
the sales data.

### The plan file is constructed, and labelled as such

TheLook ships no budget data. The planning file is generated by
`src/build_plan_source.py` from a stated, deterministic rule:

```
plan(country, month) = actual(country, same month last year)
                     × trailing_growth(country, planning cycle)
                     × ambition(country)
                     × seasonal_ambition(month)
```

`trailing_growth` is the growth rate the planner could actually observe when
writing the plan — the FY2023 plan sees CY2022/CY2021 and nothing later.
`ambition` is a per-country factor drawn once from U(0.90, 1.12) under a fixed
seed, representing the stretch or caution each market negotiates on top of
trend.

**This is a constructed planning scenario, not source data.** It is labelled
that way in the README, in the Excel pack's Definitions sheet, and in the
script's own docstring. The numbers are fully reproducible from the rule.

A first version used a flat +15% growth assumption. Against a dataset growing
70–185% a year that produced 140–210% achievement in every single market,
which made the variance analysis meaningless — nothing was ever below plan.
The trailing-growth rule replaced it, and the result is a realistic spread
with two markets landing short.

## 2. Master-data harmonisation

This part is **not** constructed. TheLook's own `users.csv` contains:

| Source spelling | Rows | Canonical country |
|---|---|---|
| `España` | 2 | Spain |
| `Deutschland` | 1 | Germany |
| `Brasil` | 14,669 | Brazil |

Left alone, a country-level report shows Spain and España as two separate
markets, each with its own plan achievement, neither correct. `seeds/country_master.csv`
maps every observed source spelling to a canonical country and a region, and
`core.dim_country` serves that mapping. Three of sixteen source values are
aliases.

Two further dimensions exist specifically for the star schema:

- `core.dim_market` — one row per canonical country. `dim_country` repeats
  `canonical_country` once per alias, so it cannot be the "one" side of a
  relationship. This distinction matters: using `dim_country` directly in
  Power BI would produce a many-to-many relationship and silently wrong
  totals.
- `core.dim_month` — one row per month, for the month-grain facts (plan,
  forecast, variance). `dim_date` is at day grain and `month_start` repeats
  within it, so the same problem applies.

## 3. Currency

Plans are submitted in local currency and converted at a **fixed budget FX
rate** set at the open of the planning cycle (`seeds/fx_budget_rates.csv`),
not at the spot rate on the day of reporting. This is standard commercial
practice: it isolates performance against plan from currency movement, so a
market that beat its target is not shown as missing it because its currency
weakened.

A real implementation would also report at constant currency and at actual
rates side by side. That is not modelled here because TheLook has no
transaction-level currency — all actuals are USD — so there is nothing
genuine to convert on the actuals side.

## 4. Validation and quarantine

`qa.plan_validation` runs twelve checks. The design decision worth stating:
**submission defects do not fail the build.**

| Behaviour | Applies to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blocking (`FAIL`) | Unmapped country, missing FX rate, broken grain, consolidation that does not tie, FX that does not reproduce, PVM residual ≠ 0 | These make the consolidated number *wrong*. Publishing is worse than stopping. |
| Non-blocking (`WARN`) | Blank cells, negative targets, late submissions, whitespace in keys | These make the plan *incomplete*. The month-end review still has to happen; the defect goes back to the region. |

Current state: **8 PASS, 4 WARN, 0 FAIL**. Of 292 submitted rows, 287 were
accepted and 5 quarantined into `qa.plan_rejects` with a stated reason.

### What the contiguity check found

`plan_month_coverage_contiguous` flags gaps in a market's own plan months. The
24 gaps it reports come from **three different root causes**, which is why the
check is worth having:

1. **Colombia (16 gaps)** — the market is too small to plan. It has 16 orders
   in total, so most months have no prior-year base to plan from. This is a
   genuine business condition, not a data error.
2. **Poland (3 gaps)** — a late submission. Two months never arrived.
3. **UK, Japan, France, Australia, Belgium (1 each)** — the quarantined rows.
   A blank or negative cell removes that month from the accepted plan, and the
   gap check is what makes that visible downstream.

A single rule surfacing three distinct causes is the argument for writing
checks against the *shape* of the data rather than against a list of known
problems.

## 5. Price-Volume-Mix bridge

The question a commercial director asks about any revenue movement is "was it
volume, price, or mix?" The bridge answers it at product-category level.

```
volume = (Q₁ − Q₀) × s₀ × P₀      growth held at prior mix and prior price
mix    = Q₁ × (s₁ − s₀) × P₀      mix shift held at prior price
price  = Q₁ᵢ × (P₁ − P₀)          price movement at current volume
```

Q is units, P is average selling price, s is the category's share of total
units in that period. The decomposition is **exact** — the three effects sum
back to the total variance, and `pvm_decomposition_is_exact` asserts a
residual below $0.01 for every comparison. That test exists because an
off-by-a-rounding bridge is the classic way this analysis quietly breaks.

Three comparisons are built: CY2023 vs CY2022, YTD Jan–Apr 2024 vs 2023, and
Apr 2024 vs Apr 2023. May 2024 is incomplete and never enters a comparison.

### What it shows

This closes a question left open in
[`analysis_and_findings.md` section 2](analysis_and_findings.md#2-revenue-and-growth):
*is growth driven by volume or basket value?* The descriptive analysis could show that
revenue was rising but could not attribute the movement. The bridge attributes it.

YTD Jan–Apr 2024 vs 2023, net sales $734,018 → $1,722,202:

| Effect | Value | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | +$972,255 | 98.4% |
| Price | +$22,107 | 2.2% |
| Mix | −$6,177 | −0.6% |
| **Total** | **+$988,185** | 100% |

ASP moved $59.59 → $60.15, under 1%. **The business is growing by selling more
units, not by charging more or by shifting into premium categories.** That is a
genuine commercial finding with a clear implication: growth is currently
volume-dependent, so it is exposed to any slowdown in traffic or conversion,
and there is unexploited headroom in price and mix.

## 6. Forecasting and validation

Three candidate methods, deliberately conventional, selected by **backtested**
accuracy rather than in-sample fit:

| Model | MAPE across horizon | 1 month out | 8 months out | Mean bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`seasonal_naive_drift`** | **8.0%** | 6.5% | 11.5% | −0.7% |
| `holt_winters_log` | 26.0% | 8.3% | 43.5% | +8.3% |
| `seasonal_naive` | 42.4% | 41.7% | 44.2% | −42.4% |

Rolling origin over **24 origins × 8 steps = 192 folds**. Each origin refits on
data available up to that point only, and every model is scored at every step
of the horizon the forecast is actually run at.

Three things worth noticing, all better discussed than hidden:

- **The simplest trend-aware method won.** Seasonal naive at 42% MAPE shows
  why — on a series growing this fast, "same month last year" is hopeless, and
  the drift term alone fixes most of it. Holt-Winters did not earn its extra
  complexity.
- **The horizon decides the answer.** At one month ahead the two serious
  candidates are 6.5% and 8.3% — close enough to argue about. At eight months
  they are 11.5% and 43.5%. An earlier version of this module scored at one
  month and ran at eight, which is how the defect in
  [`forecast_review.md`](forecast_review.md) was found.
- **Holt-Winters is the unstable one.** Its worst single fold reaches 163%
  against 39% for the champion. On a steep series, a fitted trend occasionally
  extrapolates badly, and eight months is long enough for that to compound.

### Honest limitation

TheLook is synthetic. Its growth curve is far smoother than real retail
demand, which carries promotional spikes, stockouts and competitive shocks. An
8.0% MAPE here is **optimistic** and should be read as a demonstration of the
validation method, not as an accuracy claim that transfers to a real business.

The plan the forecast is measured against is also constructed — TheLook ships
no budget, so `src/build_plan_source.py` generates one from a published rule.
The achievement rate below inherits that.

The module was held back from every presented surface until it had been
audited. [`forecast_review.md`](forecast_review.md) is that audit: seven checks
specified in advance, two of which found a defect, two of which tested a
hypothesis that turned out to be wrong, and three of which passed.

### Country allocation

Top-down: the global forecast is allocated by each market's trailing six-month
share of net sales. Bottom-up per-country models were rejected because the
small markets (Colombia, Poland) have too few observations to fit seasonality,
and a stated top-down split is more honest than thirteen weak models averaged
together.

## 7. Scenarios

Three scenarios over the remaining plan horizon, each with a stated
assumption rather than an unexplained multiplier:

| Scenario | Volume | Margin | Assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | ×1.00 | — | Current trend continues; no change to pricing or promotion intensity |
| Upside | ×1.10 | +0.5pp | Peak-season sell-through runs 10% ahead with a modest mix benefit |
| Downside | ×0.90 | −2.0pp | Demand softens 10% and clearing aged stock costs 2 points of margin |

The downside margin assumption is tied to a real finding from the inventory
analysis in [`analysis_and_findings.md` section 5](analysis_and_findings.md#5-operations-delivery-returns-and-inventory):
273K units sit in the 181+ day age bucket, and clearing them means discounting.
The scenario assumptions are deliberately sourced from the descriptive layer
rather than invented, so a reader can trace every assumption back to evidence.

## 8. Results

### Plan achievement by region

| Region | 2023 | 2024 YTD |
|---|---|---|
| AMER | 97.9% | 126.5% |
| APAC | 94.2% | 122.2% |
| EMEA | 105.9% | 140.5% |

2023 landed close to plan in all three regions — two slightly short, one
ahead. 2024 is running far ahead everywhere, because the plan was built on the
prior year's growth rate and actual growth accelerated instead. That pattern
is a property of the synthetic generator's accelerating curve, and it is worth
naming rather than presenting as commercial outperformance.

Only two markets are below plan across complete months: **Poland at 72%** and
**Colombia at 85%** — the same two markets flagged by the coverage check, which
is a useful corroboration rather than a coincidence.

### Landing the year

Expected net sales across the plan horizon, using actuals where known and the
forecast beyond, come to **119.6% of plan**.

## 9. What this dataset cannot support

Stated plainly, because a commercial analyst is asked these and the right
answer is what would be needed, not silence.

| Concept | Status | Blocking limitation | What would be needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price elasticity | Not possible | No discount or promotion field; price is effectively fixed per product, so there is no price variation to regress against | Actual selling price by week and channel, plus a promotion calendar. Method: log-log demand regression with product fixed effects, and an explicit treatment of the fact that discounts are not randomly assigned — they co-occur with display, so a naive coefficient is inflated |
| Promotion effectiveness | Not possible | No campaign table, no exposure flag, no control group | Promotion calendar with exposed and unexposed stores or customers. Method: within-product difference-in-differences against unexposed comparison, not a simple before/after |
| Marketing incrementality / ROAS | Not possible | No marketing cost and no holdout | Spend by channel and a geo or audience holdout. Method: incrementality test, or a geo-experiment where a holdout is impractical |
| Multi-touch attribution | Not possible | One `traffic_source` per session, no touchpoint sequence per user | Full cross-session touchpoint sequence keyed to a user |
| Market basket | Partial | Average basket is 1.4 items, so lift between categories is weak by construction | Larger baskets. The association rules in `data/processed/advanced/category_association_rules.csv` are computed and reported honestly as weak |
| Customer lifetime value | Possible, not built | — | Time allowed; RFM and cohort retention are built, BG/NBD would be the natural next step |
| Supplier lead time / replenishment | Not possible | No purchase orders, no supplier data | PO table with order and receipt dates |
| Constant-currency reporting on actuals | Not possible | Actuals carry no transaction currency — all USD | Transaction currency on the order line |
